Combinations at a glance

What each screen looks like with the tools in different states. The monitor is a slim strip above the piano, or above the mini-player and tab bar when there is no piano.

Decisions to approve

  • One Practice tools control replaces the Piano tab, the playback header piano button, and the viewer toolbar piano button. It opens a floating menu with a switch per tool. The menu stays open while you toggle and closes on a press outside or Escape. Show other-octave matches appears in the menu only in the sheet music viewer, where it means something.
  • Two forms of one tool. The monitor is a 64 pt strip docked above the piano (or above the mini-player and tab bar) and never covers the transport. Its expand button opens the full Pitch view, a full-screen study view. The earlier mid-size dock is gone.
  • Full Pitch view, portrait: a large readout with the zone in words, a Closeness panel (cents against time, with Precise ±10, Near ±25 and Quarter tone ±50 bands), the progression roll (note bars on a labelled scale, coloured by zone, with zoom), and a readout bar with pitch, confidence, a microphone toggle, reference tuning and state. Sensitivity and reference tuning are in a settings sheet from the header.
  • Landscape: the roll takes the width and the Closeness panel and readout sit in a column beside it, like the reference tuner.
  • Target: by default the nearest note. Target holds A3 (in the real view, a note you pick), and the trace then measures distance from it, clamping at the panel edge with an arrow when it is beyond ±50.
  • Piano and score marks as before: a filled accent key with a dot and glow; a filled notehead with halo for exact matches and a dashed ring for other-octave matches; a Following track pitch chip when the track is shifted.
  • Open question: should the Pitch view offer a freeze or pause of the display, so a take can be studied after singing it? It is not in the mock or the spec yet.
  • Headphones are assumed; the first time listening starts, one dismissible note says so.
