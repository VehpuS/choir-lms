- [x] 1.2 Web Audio check: confirm in the browser preview that a separate `AudioContext` plays over the playing track without affecting the shaping graph, that `resume()` works from the first key press, and the first-press latency with the samples cold and warm. Record the numbers here.
  - Outcome (2026-10-19, Chrome in the browser pane): the piano's own `AudioContext` is created and `resume()`d when the overlay opens (a user gesture), and is `running` by the first key press. A key press creates its oscillators 0.3 ms after the pointer-down (no sample load; the synthesized voice has nothing to fetch), with `baseLatency` 5.8 ms and `outputLatency` 24 ms. The context is separate from the shaping graph by construction (it shares nothing with the track's nodes); the over-a-playing-track check is part of 8.1. Cold/warm latency does not differ without samples; revisit with task 9.1.
- [x] 1.3 OSMD hit-testing: confirm how to map a pointer position to a notehead and its pitch (via `GraphicSheet` or the SVG note groups) at each viewer zoom step, including a MIDI-converted score and a part with `<transpose>`. Record the chosen approach in design Decision 7.
  - Outcome (2026-10-19): after each OpenSheetMusicDisplay render the renderer walks `GraphicSheet.MeasureList` and sets `data-midi` (the sounding pitch: written pitch plus the part's `<transpose>`) on every notehead element from `getNoteheadSVGs()`, skipping rests and tie continuations (`score-note-tags.ts`). The attribute survives the strip renderer copying a run's SVG into its slot, which a map keyed by element would not. A tap is answered from the DOM: the nearest tagged notehead within a 12 px slop of the pointer (`score-note-hit-test.ts`), found by `getBoundingClientRect`, so it needs no zoom maths and works at every zoom step. Checked on a MIDI-converted score (132 heads on the first run, 258 after scrolling to a later run of a long score, first pitches F♯4, G♯4, A4 matching the staff). A part with `<transpose>` is covered by a unit spec (written C5 → B♭4) and not yet by a real score.

- [x] 10.1 (added 2026-10-20 at the user's request) **The piano works with no track.** Every entry point was tied to a track (mini-player, playback screen) or the viewer, so with nothing playing the piano could not be opened. Added a `Piano` toggle item to the tab bar (`shell-tab-bar.tsx`, presentation specced in `shell-tab-bar-model.spec.ts`, filled glyph `piano-keyboard-filled`), shown wherever `pianoCapabilities().canPlay`. - [x] 10.2 (added 2026-10-20 at the user's request) **Piano sound on first use, outside playback.** Cause: browsers only start audio from a qualifying gesture, and a touch `pointerdown` is not one (Safari and touch Chrome need `pointerup`, `touchend`, or `click`); a page already playing media is exempt, which is why the piano worked during playback. The engine only called `resume()` from the key's `pointerdown`, then scheduled the note anyway on the still-frozen clock and released it (fading it from silence) before the context ever ran, so the first presses were silent or lost. Fix: `usePianoSound` now starts the context from any `pointerup`, `touchend`, `click`, or `keydown` while the piano or score taps are in use; the engine holds a press until the context is running (the key shows held; a tap that ended first still sounds for at least 200 ms, and a tap older than a second is dropped), instead of scheduling on a suspended context. Specced with a fake context whose `resume()` stays pending until a gesture allows it (`piano-sound-engine.spec.ts`). Verified in the browser by suspending and blocking the context: no oscillators on the frozen clock, each gesture retries `resume()`, and the waiting tap sounds once audio is allowed. Not verified on a real iPhone or touch Chrome, which is the case this targets.
