1 Rehearsal player — Nocturne visual refresh, structure unchanged
Assumptions and reasoning

Same features, same three destinations, same library views. What changes is the surface: the parchment-and-forest palette becomes Nocturne's dark ground, and the screens stop looking like stacked forms and start looking like a player — rows instead of cards, one large title per screen, transport where a singer's thumb already is.

Streaming apps carry their hierarchy in album art. Rehearsal tracks from Drive have none, so type carries it instead: a 15px medium title over a 12px dim meta line, monospaced times, and one accent line per screen. Nothing is filled with accent — Nocturne uses it as a line and a glow, so the play button is an accent ring, the played waveform is accent, the active tab gets a short accent mark.

Loops become first-class library items, listed beside tracks with their own range bracket, and Now Playing is two-state: practice controls collapsed, full-screen waveform on expand. The waveform is the scrubbing UI at full screen — the shapes here are a stub standing in for real audio analysis.

Speed and pitch are drawn as planned features, and as one sheet with two outcomes: adjust and keep playing (ephemeral), or commit the current settings into a new derived track or loop. Speed is a continuous multiplier that never shifts pitch, so no pitch-lock control exists; pitch moves in semitones. Tempo source is a named field so BPM entry and score-follow against MIDI or MusicXML can land later without moving the control.

The existing advanced search, scope, sort, entity filter and tag match-mode controls are carried over unchanged in behaviour — screen 1j shows them restyled, not reduced.

Icons are Phosphor per the system. Chrome is drawn from the real shell: 3-tab bar, persistent mini player, bottom-sheet playback surface, breadcrumb explorer, view-switcher chips.
